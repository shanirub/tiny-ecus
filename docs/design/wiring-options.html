Four ways to wire the wheel nodes

The problem: every wheel node needs motor power, logic power, ground and the CAN pair from a shared source. That is 4 nodes × 6 shared connections, plus about 11 local wires per node. Below are four ways to organise it. A short primer on wire thickness comes first, because it decides which wire goes where.

Primer

Wire thickness: only two places care

Thin wire has more resistance, so it gets warm and loses voltage when a lot of current flows. Voltage does not matter here; current does. In this car almost every wire carries milliamps, and only the motor power path carries amps. So you need three kinds of wire, not a catalogue.

Option A · bench only

One big breadboard backbone

All four nodes sit on one or two full-size breadboards. The two rail pairs carry the power: top for motor VBAT/GND, bottom for 5 V/GND. A spare row pair carries CANH/CANL. Motor cables plug straight in.

motor VBATGND5 V logicCANHCANLmotor cable / signals

Good

  • Fastest to start; no new parts beyond the boards.
  • Power and CAN wiring nearly disappear: short hops to the rails.
  • Everything visible and probeable in one place.

Watch

  • Bench only: motor power on rails is fine only under the PSU limit (≤ 1 A).
  • Probably needs two full-size boards (~75–80 rows for 4 nodes vs 63).
  • Skips the real CAN harness; Phase 5 starts the wiring from zero.
Option B · bench and car

Bus cable plus a power star

The automotive and drone approach. One 4-wire cable (5 V, GND, CANH, CANL) is daisy-chained from node to node with small locking connectors. Drones use exactly this with the DroneCAN 4-pin cable. Motor power goes separately as a star from two WAGO blocks, one short 22 AWG pair per motor driver. Each node is its own small board.

Good

  • Same wiring on the bench and in the car; Phase 5 only swaps breadboard for perfboard inside each node.
  • Each node has 3 external plugs: bus in, bus out, motor power. Unplug a node and carry it to the desk.
  • Correct CAN topology (line with short stubs): this is the Phase 3 learning.
  • Star power: each branch carries only its own motor.

Watch

  • Needs connectors and either a crimp tool or pre-crimped XH leads.
  • 4 small boards on the bench; on the car they need 4 mounting spots.
  • XH is 2.5 mm pitch, close to but not exactly 0.1"; fine for 4 pins on perfboard.
Option C · solder once, plug in modules

Backplane board

Like a PC motherboard: one stripboard (copper strips along each row) with four identical node slots made of female headers. The C3, DRV8833 and CAN module of each node plug into their slot. Power and CAN run along the board as copper strips, so there are no loose power or bus wires at all. Motor cables plug into a 6-pin header at each slot.

Good

  • Fewest loose wires of all; very robust against vibration.
  • Modules stay replaceable: they sit in sockets.
  • Compact: one board on the upper deck.

Watch

  • Soldering up front, before the pin choices of Phases 1–3 are known; a pin change means cutting strips and rewiring.
  • VBAT strip carries all four motors: check strip width or add a 20 AWG wire along it.
  • Nodes are central, so motor cables run from each corner to the middle. The 6-wire cables may be too short.
Option D · a placement variant of B

Two side pods

Group the two nodes of each side on one board: a left pod (FL + RL) between the left wheels and a right pod (FR + RR) between the right wheels. Inside a pod the two nodes share 5 V, GND and CAN by a few short wires; between pods there is one 4-wire bus cable and one power pair. Seen from above on the flipped chassis:

Good

  • Only 2 boards: fits the "no room for 4 mini breadboards" constraint.
  • Short motor cables; nodes sit next to their wheels, close to the ECU idea.
  • Fewer bus hops (gateway → left → right) than four separate nodes.

Watch

  • Two nodes share a board, so swapping one means handling both.
  • Space between the wheels depends on the middle-gap measurement (F.5).
  • A half-size breadboard per pod is tight; check with real modules.

Decision · 2026-10-15

Chosen: D, two side pods, wired the B way

D answers the space problem, keeps motor cables short, and still uses B's two good ideas: the 4-wire bus cable and the power star. It also carries over from bench to car without a rebuild. If the middle gap in F.5 leaves no room for pods between the wheels, fall back to C on the upper deck.

Assumptions behind the counts below: one H-bridge per motor, one corner LED per node, bus order gateway → left pod → right pod (Phase 3 confirms it). Motor power enters each pod at a small WAGO 221-413 (in, driver 1, driver 2), so it never runs along a breadboard strip.

AWG (American Wire Gauge): a bigger number means a thinner wire. 20 AWG is thicker than 22, which is thicker than 26.
